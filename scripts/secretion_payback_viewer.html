What does a secretion repay?

One cell pays to deposit a catalyst into a shared material. Does it recover the cost? Do its three neighbors gain? Compare a programmed secretion pulse with a matched branch that secretes nothing, then look at the actual material left behind.

Three founder banks, four stationary cells per bank, closed chemistry and no division. Each pulse/control pair starts from the same physical checkpoint. These are programmed interventions, with no learning or cooperation claim.

What changes, and what is held fixed?

All other secretion is suppressed in both branches. Movement, growth and division are disabled; ordinary uptake, metabolism, catalytic conversion, adsorption, diffusion and action costs still run through the shared Julia schedule. Raw-available and raw-absent conditions are separate matched pairs. The raw-available material can repay an investment that does not repay without raw substrate.

Private payoff means the producer’s usable potential difference. Neighbor benefit means the sum for three other residents. Shared benefit from a forced pulse does not establish cooperation, a learned policy, or a stable ecological strategy. Founder banks are the replication level; four residents and two substrate conditions are nested observations.
