# The light takes the long way

A warm, twisting mineral carries fine blue paths through its interior. Three fixed apertures illuminate a material which gradually changes their destinations. This revisits the older GRIN checkerboard study as a spatial artwork, retaining its Julia eikonal tracer rather than drawing plausible curves.

`studies/refractive_memory.jl` composes the existing `RecurrentMaterial` process and `src/grin.jl`. Three authored helical mineral/nutrient lenses contain phase-textured eight-channel recurrence. Conservative writing, erosion and diffusion change the solid fraction. The initial helices are designed, not claimed to emerge from noise. No training takes place. Optical observation is one-way: this recording does not feed absorbed light into the material.

The native run is 256³ over a 64³ material domain, dt 0.025, 960 steps, with five saved states at t=0,6,12,18,24. The producer checks its diffusion stability limit and finite, nonnegative fields. Complete production including captures took 35.180 seconds on local Metal; final material mass residual was 3.04e-10 relative to initial stock. This is a bounded production measurement, not an isolated kernel benchmark or asymptotic speed claim.

For optical observation the cube maps to [-1,1]³. Index is 1 + 0.65 × solid × boundary taper × (0.94 + 0.06(550/λ)²). The smooth boundary taper returns the index to one. The wavelength law is an artistic Cauchy-like choice, not a calibrated mineral. Three small disk apertures at z=-0.65 launch along fixed helical tangents; receiver z=1.05 is unchanged across the recording. Cell-centered trilinear interpolation and its piecewise gradient feed the existing midpoint eikonal integrator. No scattering, interference, Fresnel splitting or physical emission is solved.

Each of five frozen states traces 21,696 equal-weight rays per wavelength (460,550,630 nm), plus 621 retained display paths. All 325,440 receiver rays reach the receiver; in-window + outside + unresolved counts equal launch counts separately for every wavelength/state. The constant-index control differs from analytic straight lines by at most 1.70e-13 optical units. Sixteen deterministic launches per wavelength/state are repeated at ds=0.001,0.0005,0.00025; maximum ds-to-half-ds endpoint difference is 0.001017, below the 0.009375 receiver pixel width. Individual piecewise-gradient refinements need not improve monotonically; this is a sampled endpoint sensitivity check, not a global error bound. The fixed 621 launches change endpoints by mean 0.273809 and maximum 0.851951 between t0 and t12.

The 2000², 128-sample Cycles photograph uses native t12 solid contours at 0.14 and 0.50, with transparent amber skin and copper inclusions. Thin cyan polylines are actual traced paths, clipped to solid support; discontinuous clipped pieces remain separate. Their brightness is a visual glyph, not computed scattering radiance. Cycles surface refraction stages the sculpture and is distinct from the numerical GRIN paths.

`build_refractive_viewer.py` reuses the shared volume viewer. It includes all five states as block means to 64³ with fixed 8-bit scaling, all 207 saved 550nm trajectories per state with every eighth saved vertex plus endpoints, and lossless receiver counts. Path coordinates use signed 16-bit quantization; maximum per-axis rounding is 3.50e-5 optical units. Fields and ray payloads are losslessly gzip-packed after display reduction. The offline HTML is 2,941,073 bytes. Rotate, zoom, cut, scrub, select paths, or compare the recorded constant-index controls. This is exploration of saved data; it does not recompute physics or interpolate time. Browser QA checked reference/cut pixel changes, state4 receiver counts, zero WebGL errors, and 390px layout without overflow.

Production lives outside Git in `outputs/collection-20261016/refractive-memory`: full Float32 fields, source snapshot and hashes, observer/tracer source per optical pass, native PNG/Blender scene, compact `optical-summary.json`, and publication review metadata. Every observer pins its input solid, producer manifest, tracer and observer hashes. The recipe and source are versioned; large states and scenes remain local.

Reproduce with Julia's `gpu` project and Metal using `studies/refractive_memory.jl produce experiments/art/refractive-memory.toml OUTPUT`, then `INDEX_CONTRAST=.65 RAY_DS=.0005 OPTICS_TAG=guided-final BEAM_MODE=helical RAY_GRID=9 SCREEN_GRID=96` and the study's `observe OUTPUT 0,240,480,720,960` command. Use the retained `render-job.json` for exact photograph settings, and `scripts/build_refractive_viewer.py OUTPUT` for the replay (NumPy required; Python packages observations only).
