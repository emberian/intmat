# A field writes a tree

`DielectricGrowth` is a CPU lattice dielectric-breakdown surrogate. An occupied conductive seed is driven at nondimensional potential one. The exterior box is grounded at zero. A successive-over-relaxation solve finds the harmonic potential between them. Growth changes the conductor geometry, and the next solve changes the field: branches screen one another rather than following a prerecorded path.

Each growth event chooses one face-connected, unoccupied frontier site with probability proportional to the **sum of incident conductor-face field magnitudes raised to eta**. The field estimate on a face is `(1 - potential_at_candidate) / cell_spacing`. Applying the exponent per face avoids an artificial preference for filling sites touching several conductor faces. A SplitMix64 hash of the fixed seed and event number supplies the random variate. The process stores its seed and counter; it never consumes a global RNG.

The explicit event clock defaults to one event per .05 units of process time. Calling `advance!` accumulates elapsed time and executes all due events, so dividing a timestep does not change the growth sequence (apart from the documented Float32 cadence tolerance). Requested events are an experiment duration, not a hidden growth-site budget. The tree stops when a conducting site becomes face-adjacent to the grounded box. This marks electrical connection across that final face; the model does not calculate subsequent current or heating.

Each occupied site, including the constructor's seed, consumes .8 units of local nutrient and adds .8 deposit. Failed constructor field convergence leaves the world untouched. Existing fixed-potential conductor cells remain at one, free cells satisfy the normalized discrete harmonic equation to tolerance 2e-5, and outer faces remain zero. This is an externally driven geometric electrical model, not an energy-conserving plasma solver or a calibrated lightning prediction. The process owns its nonperiodic potential, occupancy and frontier arrays even though the shared World grid's other processes use periodic boundaries.

Run `julia --project --threads=2 bin/interior.jl study electrical outputs/electrical/tree 40 280`. The selected seed29/eta3 specimen naturally stops at event258 (259 occupied sites) when it reaches ground. `outputs/electrical/tree/volume` is its volume export; `frame-0258-*` preserves nutrient, deposit, frontier activity and potential. Final normalized potential residual is 2.60e-6. The measured nutrient+deposit mass difference is only floating point roundoff.

Optical density adds a dilute amber carrier; direct deposit supplies the channel. A six-neighbor one-cell glow halo is an optical transfer only, and a spherical viewing mask crops the computational box. These are rendering choices, not new simulated branches. The deposit `projection.png` shows the field-grown branching topology. The renderer can photograph it with gold channel emission.

Tests in `test/electrical.jl` check: harmonic residual and maximum principle; an independent direct linear-system harmonic benchmark; conductor connectivity; exact deposit budget and nutrient conservation; reproducibility including timestep partition; invalid mutable controls before any staged mutation; owned-state array shapes; and constructor failure atomicity. All25 assertions passed on Julia1.12.4 CPU.

`channels.obj` exports occupied cell centers and every occupied face adjacency: the exact conductive graph used by the potential boundary geometry. It is not a unique parent/child growth lineage (each event's probability combines every incident conducting face). Rendering these edges as small cylinders is a sharper optical interpretation of that graph; cylinder radius must be declared as styling rather than simulated channel thickness.
