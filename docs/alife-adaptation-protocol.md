# Small funded adaptation study — preregistered 15 October 2026

This independent study tests whether inherited descendants outperform their actual initial founder pool after all acquired state and material carryover are reset. It does not alter either live arena. The fixed configuration is `experiments/alife/adaptation.toml`; implementation is `studies/alife_adaptation.jl`.

Selection uses seeds 11, 29, 47, complementary half-domain versus uniform sources, and mutation enabled versus disabled: twelve runs. The model is the existing small metabolic comparison (24² grid, 24 initial founders, inherited controllers and heterogeneous allocation, capacity 180), with lifespan 80, uptake 1.4, dt .2 and a declared selection horizon t=1600 (20 lifespans). Each substrate receives a 100,000-unit finite source budget, exceeding twice its worst-case horizon supply. This is externally funded selection, not a closed-energy persistence claim. Mutation-enabled runs mutate both controller and metabolic traits. No-mutation runs still permit founder sorting and within-life plasticity.

Primary assay outcome is terminal change in descendant-to-founder log frequency: `log((living_descendants + .5)/(living_founders + .5))`, from equal initial group sizes. The .5 correction makes single-group extinction finite for display; report both group counts and extinction alongside every effect. Both-extinct assays provide no positive adaptation evidence even though their corrected log ratio is zero. Extinct selection populations remain explicit failed-persistence outcomes and are not silently removed from selection summaries.

Secondary outcomes are cumulative births and deaths by group, final population, work spent on reproduction, movement, signal, remodeling and film; resource consumption and supplied/remaining chemical mass; total material and chemical-energy residuals; controller/metabolic variance; founder effective count; and maximum living generation. Births and deaths are counted at every physical step from inherited group labels, including descendants later lost before a sampled observation. No terminal-only abundance is substituted for cumulative reproduction.

Each selection population is assayed in both source geometries, with two independent position/sampling replicates and plasticity rates .03 and zero. Every assay disables controller/metabolic mutation. Thirty-two genotypes are sampled **with replacement** from descendants and thirty-two from the original founder pool. This permits equal starts after strong bottlenecks; resulting replicates are not independent evolutionary replicates. Both groups receive fresh random positions, age zero, storage .4 and energy 1; recurrent hidden state and plastic marks are zero. Fresh resource/medium/phase fields remove niche and maternal material carryover. The plasticity-zero assay measures inherited performance without reacquired plastic marks; the plasticity-enabled assay measures inherited performance including ability to learn anew. Assay capacity is 512, leaving room above its 64-organism start; capacity blocking is recorded. The assay horizon t=320 is four founder lifespans, long enough that founding individuals cannot explain terminal survival by initial lifespan alone.

Life-only null assays draw both labels from the same exact founder pool, with mutation/plasticity disabled, in both environments and two position replicates. They measure position, stochastic founder sampling and numerical/ID-order effects. They are controls for this assay, not an artificial no-reproduction organism.

All conditions, horizons and metrics above are fixed before the full run. Report individual seed/geometry/mutation/plasticity effects and counts, plus the life-only distribution. With three evolutionary seeds, inference is descriptive; no significance claim, open-ended evolution claim, or adaptive-cooperation conclusion follows from this study. A consistent descendant advantage beyond life-only imbalance supports heritable performance improvement in these conditions; a home-versus-away difference supports geometry-specific adaptation. Null, mixed or negative effects are informative outcomes.

Every run retains append-only scalar and lineage histories and a final full serialized state. The initial founder genotype pool is serialized separately; implementation hashes and configuration accompany results. Float32 conservation residuals are measured, not assumed zero.

Interpretation notes from implementation review: the terminal selection pool samples living genotypes in proportion to their abundance, not all genotypes that ever appeared. Assay group labels replace original founder ancestry, so the assay's generic `founder_effective_count` field measures only two-label balance and must not be interpreted as founder or genotype diversity. Likewise selection `living_groups` counts only original founder labels 1 and 2, not the selection population; use that field only in assays. True selection founder diversity remains in the selection `founder_effective_count` metric. Capacity-blocked birth counts and terminal group stored energy/material are retained in full checkpoints and extracted with `scripts/inspect_adaptation_checkpoints.jl`; their omission from the scalar stream does not require rerunning the experiment.

Assay “founder extinction” means extinction of the **ancestral-genotype group including all its descendants**. The group label is inherited through every birth. It does not mean merely that initial assay individuals reached their lifespan: that necessarily happens to both initial groups before the four-lifespan endpoint. All offspring in the assay inherit the transplanted genotype with deliberate mutation disabled.
